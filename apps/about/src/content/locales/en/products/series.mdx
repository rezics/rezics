## Core purpose

A series answers which works belong to the same long-running context, not which books one person grouped together. Each member retains its own identity, source, language, release, and content; the series only establishes an intelligible relationship.

For web novels and light novels, a series can include main stories, sequels, side stories, different generations, and even works with their own platform sources and publication versions.

## What is available now

1. Create series identities with localized names and summaries.
1. Connect books, media, software, and releases to a series.
1. View other members from a work.
1. Present traceable releases on a series detail page.
1. Let communities maintain relationships without rewriting members.

## Deliberate first-version boundary

The first version centers on members and release-version relationships; it does not enable content structure for a series. Complex ordering, branches, and interpretation can first be described in posts, avoiding the premature forcing of every series into one tree structure before clear product interactions exist.

Canonical/variant relationships do not remove a variant's capabilities. Communities can prioritize maintaining the canonical entry's series, while variants still participate in relationships through the same mechanism and, when needed, refer to works related to the canonical entry.

## In development

Real cases will determine the needed structure for formal order, branches, derivative series, and cross-media series. Until then, relationships remain simple, explainable, and free of false guarantees.

## Relationships and boundaries

A series is not a release, collection, tag, or content catalogue. Releases describe an occurrence, collections express user organization, tags classify, and content structure orders content.
