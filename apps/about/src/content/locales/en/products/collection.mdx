## A good booklist should not stop with people who speak its creator’s language

What readers share is the judgment that these works belong together, not a string of titles legible in only one language. If a Japanese reader curates ten works, Chinese- or German-speaking readers should still understand the selection, find a source they can read, and retain the original order and explanation.

Cross-language booklists turn one act of curation into a product that can keep circulating. The curator can focus on choosing works; everyone else need not translate the whole list or rebuild a disconnected copy first.

## One act of curation, naturally presented for each reader

A list member stores a stable work identity, not the title that happened to be on screen when it was added. When someone opens the list, work names, summaries, and available sources can be presented for their language and region while the curator’s choices, order, explanation, and publication context stay intact.

When a new translated title or source is added later, existing lists benefit as well. Curatorial value accumulates instead of splitting into lists that cannot recognize one another across languages and platforms.

## The products that complete this experience together

1. Collections preserve the list’s own name, explanation, visibility, order, and structural history.
1. Units make every member point to the same stable work rather than one translated title or URL.
1. Books, releases, and source relationships help readers reach a presentation and reading entry that fits them.
1. Realms and feeds place a list in a real community context where it can be shared, discussed, and rediscovered.
1. Tags, libraries, and progress connect public curation back to personal discovery, saving, and reading.

These are not add-ons beside the booklist. Together they determine whether the list can genuinely cross languages and keep creating value.

## Available now, and still growing

People can already create collections, manage members and order, set multilingual presentation and visibility, open public detail pages, publish to a Realm, and preserve published structural revisions. That is enough to support the core cross-language booklist flow.

More automatic URL resolution, missing-translation prompts, local source suggestions, dynamic collections, and additional presentation modes remain planned or in development. The product distinguishes clearly between the experience available now and the fuller one it is working toward.

## The foundation is a Collection; the product is not limited to books

Cross-language booklists are the most focused form of Collections and the clearest place to see their connected value. The same foundation can support watchlists, game lists, and community curation. They share stable members, order, publication, and history while each can become a complete product of its own.

A Collection is not a series, tag result, or file folder. A series describes formal relationships among works, and tags provide classification vocabulary; a Collection preserves why its creator placed these works together.
