## Core purpose

In REZICS, “everything is a Unit” does not mean everything is the same. Every object that must persist receives a shared capability set: stable ID, multilingual names, visibility, state, relationships, permissions, and traceable history. Books, media, software, posts, Realms, and people add their own fields on that foundation.

For web novels, this resolves a practical problem: platform URLs, original titles, translations, and publication listings can all change, while the reader is still looking for the same work.

## What is available now

1. Create a stable work identity independent of any single language or platform.
1. Preserve multilingual names, summaries, aliases, and localized presentation.
1. Connect series, releases, entities, tags, sources, and other Unit relationships.
1. Point content, collections, progress, reviews, and Realms at the same object.
1. Use state, visibility, permissions, and governance rules to decide who may read or change it.

## Canonical records, variants, and cross-platform sources

REZICS does not claim it can define one unique, absolute “original” for every work. The product uses canonical-record and variant relationships: a community can agree on a primary identity while other language editions, platform editions, or clear variants keep their full capabilities and point to it.

A source URL says where an object appears; it does not replace work identity. People collect, review, and publish into a Realm about the work, not an unscoped URL.

## In development

Large work networks need duplicate detection, Unit merge and split, external authority identifiers, source evidence, bulk imports, permanent post-merge redirects, and cross-language name disambiguation. These capabilities directly determine data quality after the initial 400,000-book catalogue.

## Relationships and boundaries

A Unit is neither a genre tag nor generic JSON. Shared fields belong on Units; only fields truly unique to a product belong on models such as books, posts, and Realms. A name is not identity, and a source is not proof of ownership.
