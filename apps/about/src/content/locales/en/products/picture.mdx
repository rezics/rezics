## Core purpose

A picture post represents a publication whose primary content is one or more ordered images. Ordinary articles can also insert images, but picture-post-specific validation, list previews, detail layouts, and pagination behavior are needed only when the image sequence itself constitutes the work.

A picture post remains both a Post and a Unit. It can therefore directly reuse titles, multilingual descriptions, authors, time, tags, replies, collections, licenses, sources, and Realm publication context.

## Established content rules

1. Include at least one image; single-image and multi-image posts use the same model.
1. Multiple images have a stable order specified by the author.
1. Each image can have an asset reference, alt text, and an optional description.
1. The first image can serve as the default list preview, without rushing to add an independent cover.
1. Semantics such as illustration, photography, comics, or screenshots are described by tags rather than adding more Post types.

## Boundaries with other content

An article with images remains an ordinary Post. A review containing many screenshots remains a review. A comic chapter is first a chapter in a book's Content Structure. Use a picture post only when there is no stronger product role and the image sequence is the subject of the publication.

A multipage illustration can be one picture post. An art book or comic with an overall title, table of contents, and reading order is a book. A group of image works collected by a user is a collection.

## In development

Image-sequence editing, upload validation, alt-text workflows, image-first content-feed cards, detail views, and multipage reading still need to be completed. The capability map therefore marks picture posts as “In development” rather than claiming availability from a complete data design alone.

## Relationships and boundaries

Underlying image assets are not Units; they preserve files, dimensions, and formats. A picture post is the publication identity that can be collected, reviewed, tagged, and connected to external sources, so parallel image or gallery Units are not created.
