# Native identity merge and reconciliation

The platform can merge two native Catalog identities with the same owner, exact shape, lifecycle status, visibility and content rating. Participating or Auth-controlled Entities are ineligible. A request pins both revisions, timestamps and its reconciliation policy; two different current human accounts other than the proposer must approve that exact fingerprint. Any rejection vetoes the request. Changed identity revisions require a new proposal.

After approval a leased worker archives the source and installs an immutable canonical edge. The source identity, child structures, semantic statements, all native history and source epochs remain under their original keys. New source writes are rejected. The canonical target can continue to receive ordinary authorized edits. Public former slug addresses resolve to the canonical target, and the target Catalog API exposes a paginated retained-sources endpoint. Retained source reads follow the current target audience only under the explicitly reviewed `target_readers` policy, with current rating, moderation and source visibility checks; private operator authority is never included in that endpoint.

The reviewed plan selects copying alternate names or retaining names; copying identifier claims or retaining identifiers; and paused source binding rebind or paused retention at the source. Name copies retain language, origin, spoiler, scope and derivation history, and never replace the target primary name. Identifier copies remain claims. Every copy carries an exact source and target native history receipt. Derivation anchors are copied as withdrawn evidence, bounded to eight links; deeper chains require an operator decision. Semantic statements and owner-specific structures are explicitly retained on the source, discoverable through the retained-sources API. This workflow does not claim that distinct Work, Episode, Edition, track, statement or membership graphs have become one structure.

Source rebind appends the native binding revision and pauses automatic import. Existing target facts are not relabeled as source-authored, and existing source adoption epochs are not moved. Further adoption on the target uses the ordinary reviewed source-proposal process. A failed name, identifier or binding operation creates an action-required item. An operator can retry against a current target revision or explicitly retain it with a reason. Completion requires every inventoried item to be applied or explicitly retained. A canonical edge can already exist while reconciliation still requires action; those states are separate in the API.

## Workload and limits

Identity lookup uses two locator/owner primary-key probes. Reviews are a database-enforced maximum of two per request and rules are bounded to 32 at the API. List endpoints use UUID keysets, with at most 100 requests/items and 20 retained-source previews per request. They hydrate review/operation/rule/visibility data in bounded batches.

The durable worker has 64 assignable shards, at most four leases per claim, a 60-second lease, a 25-second SQL statement limit and five-second lock limit. It mutates at most eight names, identifiers or bindings per page, or inventories at most 128 semantic heads. Each selected name can require at most nine origin nodes including its root; the dependency recursion cap produces action-required evidence instead of unbounded work. It advances indexed `(owner_id,id)` or `(owner_id,mapping_key,source_record_id)` keysets and commits the cursor with receipts. A lost lease cannot commit another worker's page. Outstanding items are paginated and survive process restarts; retry backoff is capped at ten minutes plus jitter, with 12 automatic attempts before operator action.

At 500 million reconciliation items, assuming 500-900 bytes of heap/TOAST plus 300-700 bytes across local indexes, plan for roughly 400-800 GB before replicas, WAL and free-space reserve. At three billion items the corresponding estimate is 2.4-4.8 TB. These are sizing estimates, not measured physical storage. Reconciliation items partition by request into 64 hash families: average 7.8 million / 46.9 million rows per partition at those baselines. A single large owner remains one sequential keyset stream but never requires loading its corpus in memory. At 50 pages/second and eight mutations/page a 500-million-item full-copy workload takes about 14.5 days before contention or origin work; three billion takes about 86.8 days. Inventory-only throughput at the same assumed page rate is 6,400 rows/second. These rates are targets to benchmark, not achieved results.

The operational thresholds are lease age, oldest eligible job age, SQL p95 page time, retry rate, outstanding item count and WAL/replication lag. Scale by assigning disjoint shards to workers and moving request partitions to additional database capacity; preserve exact native-history routing for receipt foreign keys. Cold completed receipts can move with their full request partition after a separately reviewed archival cutover. No request or recurring maintenance operation scans all receipts or rewrites source foreign keys. Representative EXPLAIN and contention fixtures are required before production throughput qualification.

## Verification boundary

Pure contract tests cover native compatibility, pinned fingerprints, review input and bounded retry/phase policy. The isolated native merge fixture must exercise actual independent account review, a multi-page worker, source preservation, exact receipt keys, write fencing, conflict resolution and finalization on the generated baseline. No browser or visual acceptance is implied.
